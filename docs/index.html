Communication-Efficient Federated Learning through Adaptive Weight Clustering and Server-Side Distillation

Compress both directions of federated training.

Vasileios Tsouvalas · Aaqib Saeed · Tanir Ozcelebi · Nirvana Meratnia

At a glance

Compress both directions of federated training. Clustering makes client uploads compact, but averaging can undo that structure. FedCompress restores it with server-side distillation and adjusts cluster capacity using an unlabeled representation score.

What this adds

  • 01

    Dual compression: clustered local training plus server self-compression after aggregation.

  • 02

    Adaptive capacity: use embedding effective rank to guide the number of shared weight values.

  • 03

    Vision and audio evidence: report communication, model size and device inference separately.

Abstract

Federated Learning (FL) is a promising technique for the collaborative training of deep neural networks across multiple devices while preserving data privacy. Despite its potential benefits, FL is hindered by excessive communication costs due to repeated server-client communication during training. To address this challenge, model compression techniques, such as sparsification and weight clustering are applied, which often require modifying the underlying model aggregation schemes or involve cumbersome hyperparameter tuning, with the latter not only adjusts the model's compression rate but also limits model's potential for continuous improvement over growing data. In this paper, we propose FedCompress, a novel approach that combines dynamic weight clustering and server-side knowledge distillation to reduce communication costs while learning highly generalizable models. Through a comprehensive evaluation on diverse public datasets, we demonstrate the efficacy of our approach compared to baselines in terms of communication costs and inference speed.

How it works

FedCompress

01

Learn task and compression together

Each client minimizes cross-entropy plus a weight-clustering penalty. The local warm-up uses β=0 before enabling clustering with β=1.

Lk=Lce+βLwc(θk,μ,C)

Equation 1, PDF page 3. μ denotes learnable centroids; C is their count.

02

Restore clusters after aggregation

Keep the underlying aggregation rule. Then optimize a clustered student to match the temperature-scaled teacher outputs on unlabeled auxiliary data held by the server.

Ls=LKL(pT∥p)+βLwc

Equation 2, PDF page 4, with model arguments abbreviated. The KL term includes the paper’s temperature scaling.

03

Measure representation diversity

Normalize the singular values of local embeddings and compute their effective rank. This is an unlabeled proxy for representation quality, not a generalization guarantee.

E=exp(−∑jrjlogrj)

Dynamic weight-clustering section, PDF page 4. r is the normalized singular-value spectrum; the implementation description adds a small stability constant.

04

Use the score to adjust capacity

Start with a small cluster count and increase it when score progress stalls. The prose uses a three-round window and patience, with an optional maximum cluster budget.

PDF page 4. The schedule monitors a three-round moving window with a patience of three; cluster capacity grows within the configured budget.

Takeaways

  • Compression must survive aggregation. Restoring the global centroid structure is the central step.
  • Communication has an accuracy cost. Table 1 reports 3.80–5.41× reductions with 0.31–1.88 percentage-point losses.
  • The server does extra work. Self-compression needs auxiliary data and optimization; inference speedups do not establish training-time or energy savings.
Study scope & sources
  • 20 clients and 20 rounds; small vision and audio models.
  • Two trials without tabulated uncertainty; individual table entries take precedence over inconsistent prose averages.
Data
CIFAR-10/100 and PathMNIST for vision; SpeechCommands and VoxForge for audio.
Models
ResNet-20 for vision; MobileNet for audio; standard FedAvg aggregation.
Auxiliary server data
StyleGAN (Oriented) for vision and LibriSpeech for audio; unlabeled data supports teacher–student self-compression.
Compared with
FedAvg, FedZip with 15 clusters, and FedCompress without server self-compression; inference on Pixel 6, Jetson Nano and Coral TPU.

Scope & limitations

  • The main training study uses 20 clients and 20 rounds; it does not establish scalability to foundation models or large client populations.
  • Server self-compression requires auxiliary data and additional optimization; sensitivity to the auxiliary distribution is not broadly quantified.
  • Accuracy averages use two trials without tabulated uncertainty, and some prose averages conflict with the reported table entries.
  • Inference acceleration is relative and modest; the paper does not directly measure end-to-end training latency or energy savings.
